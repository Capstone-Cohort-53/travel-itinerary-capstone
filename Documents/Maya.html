Destination Wedding Organizer Persona

Persona

Name: Maya Rodriguez

Technology:

    Cell Phone Apple iPhone 15 Pro
    Desktop: Apple MacBook Air 13-inch (M2)
    Watch: Apple Watch Series 9

Proficiency

Maya is moderately high to highly proficient with technology. She’s 29, regularly uses travel sites, social media, group chats, and online booking, but she’s not a technical professional.

Maya is comfortable navigating websites, mobile apps, social media, online booking platforms, and digital planning tools. She learns new apps quickly but prefers technology that is intuitive and does not require instructions.

She wants to do the destination wedding planning on her Macbook and use her iPhone during the actual trip for the itinerary, directions, reservations, messages, and last-minute changes.

Love/Hate

Love

    Planning memorable experiences for friends and family
    Beautiful destinations and unique activities
    Having everything organized ahead of time
    Finding venues and churches on Instagram/TikTok

Hate

    Answering the same questions over and over
    Chasing people for RSVPs or decisions
    Last-minute cancellations and changes
    Information scattered between texts, emails, and group chats

Attitudes and Behaviors

Maya enjoys planning ahead and keeping her destination wedding organized, even when all the details are not finalized. She likes researching venues, churches, and activities and prefers to manage the larger planning tasks on her MacBook.

Maya wants to keep her bridal party informed as plans develop without sharing unfinished details with every guest. She frequently uses her iPhone for group messages and quick updates and prefers having one central place for wedding information instead of searching through texts, emails, and notes.

User Story

As the organizer of a destination wedding, I want to add the church and reception locations to my itinerary before the times are finalized so that I can keep my bridal party updated as the wedding plans develop.

Use Case

Scenario

Maya has confirmed the church and reception locations for her destination wedding, but the times are not finalized. She wants to add both locations to the wedding itinerary and share the preliminary information with her bridal party.

Precondition

Maya has an account, has created her wedding trip, and has added her bridal party to the trip.

Postcondition

The church and reception locations are saved to the itinerary without required times, and Maya's bridal party can view the updated information.

Interaction Flow

  1. User Interaction: Maya logs into her account.
  2. Website Response: The website authenticates Maya and displays her dashboard.
  3. User Interaction: Maya selects her destination wedding trip.
  4. Website Response: The website opens the wedding itinerary.
  5. User Interaction: Maya selects Add Event.
  6. Website Response: The website displays a form to create a new itinerary event.
  7. User Interaction: Maya enters the church name and location but leaves the time blank.
  8. Website Response: The website accepts the location information without requiring a time.
  9. User Interaction: Maya selects Bridal Party as the audience.
  10. Website Response: The website sets the event visibility to bridal-party members only.
  11. User Interaction: Maya selects Save Event.
  12. Website Response: The website saves the church event and displays it on the itinerary.
  13. User Interaction: Maya repeats the process to add the reception location.
  14. Website Response: The website saves the reception event and displays it on the itinerary.
  15. User Interaction: Maya views the updated wedding itinerary.
  16. Website Response: The website displays the church and reception locations to the bridal party and shows that the event times are not yet determined.
