Destination Celebration Organizer Persona

Persona

Name: Maya Rodriguez

Technology:

Devices

    Cell Phone Apple iPhone 15 Pro
    Desktop: Apple MacBook Air 13-inch (M2)
    Watch: Apple Watch Series 9

Proficiency

Devices

Maya is moderately high to highly proficient with technology. She’s 29, regularly uses travel sites, social media, group chats, and online booking, but she’s not a technical professional.

Maya is comfortable navigating websites, mobile apps, social media, online booking platforms, and digital planning tools. She learns new apps quickly but prefers technology that is intuitive and does not require instructions.

She wants to do wedding planning on her Macbook and use her iPhone during the actual trip for the itinerary, directions, reservations, messages, and last-minute changes.

Love/Hate

Love

    Planning memorable experiences for friends and family
    Beautiful destinations and unique activities
    Having everything organized ahead of time
    Finding restaurants, hotels, and activities on Instagram/TikTok

Hate

    Answering the same questions over and over
    Chasing people for RSVPs or decisions
    Last-minute cancellations and changes
    Information scattered between texts, emails, and group chats

Attitudes and Behaviors

Maya begins planning months in advance and spends significant time researching destinations and comparing options. She uses her laptop for detailed planning and relies on Google, travel websites, Instagram, and TikTok to find hotels, restaurants, activities, and inspiration.

She creates group chats, shares links, sends screenshots, creates polls, and may use spreadsheets or shared documents to keep track of important information. She wants everyone to have input on certain decisions, but she also needs to maintain enough control to keep the trip organized.

Maya understands that not everyone in the group will have the same budget, schedule, or interests. Some travelers may arrive on different days, stay at different accommodations, or choose not to participate in every activity.

Once the trip begins, Maya relies heavily on her phone to access the itinerary, reservation details, addresses, and group messages. Because she organized the trip, people continue to contact her whenever they have questions or need information.

Frustrations and Needs

Maya's biggest frustration is having important trip information scattered across multiple places. Reservation confirmations may be stored in emails, activity ideas may be saved on social media, flight information may be sent through text messages, and important decisions may disappear inside a busy group chat.

She becomes frustrated when she has to answer the same questions repeatedly. Travelers may ask, "What hotel are we staying at?", "What time is dinner?", "How much do I owe?", "Who's going to this activity?", or "What are we doing tomorrow?"

Coordinating such a large group also means dealing with late responses, changing group sizes, different budgets, transportation needs, and people who forget deadlines. When plans change, Maya needs an easy way to make sure everyone receives the updated information.

Maya needs one centralized place where she can organize the itinerary, activities, accommodations, transportation information, reservation details, and group participation. She also needs a simple way for travelers to RSVP, vote on activities, and access information without constantly contacting her.

Goals

Maya wants to create an unforgettable celebration without turning the planning process into a second full-time job.

Before the trip, she wants to create a shared itinerary, invite travelers, organize accommodations and activities, track RSVPs, collect group preferences, and clearly communicate important deadlines and trip information.

She wants members of the group to have enough flexibility to manage their own travel arrangements while still having one central location where everyone can see the overall plan.

During the trip, Maya wants every traveler to be able to quickly see what the group is doing, where they need to be, when they need to arrive, and who is participating. When something changes, she wants to update the information once instead of contacting 15–30 people individually.

Most importantly, Maya wants to enjoy the celebration with her friends and family instead of spending the entire trip acting as the group's travel agent, accountant, event planner, and customer service representative.

User Story

As the organizer of a destination celebration for a large group, I need one place to organize and share our itinerary, reservations, activities, and important trip information so everyone can stay informed without constantly asking me for updates.
